Formula One Grand Prix in the browser

MicroProse's 1991 game, unchanged, running in an emulator. A new renderer reads the game's state as it runs and draws the 3D view at your screen's full resolution and width. The handling, the other drivers and the timing are the original game's; the sound is the Amiga version's.

Play: modernWidescreen, every car in 3D with rolling wheels and a shadow, real rear views in the mirrors, a smoothed cockpit. Play: classicDrawn as the original: its framing, its picture cars, its haze. Live mapThe game beside a top-down map of every car.

Playing

  • The game loads in a few seconds (about 4 MB). The menus are the game's own screens. When you are in the car the new view takes over the screen, with the game's cockpit, dash, mirrors and messages over it.
  • Sound starts on your first key press or click. It is the Amiga version's: Dave Lowe's original title tune in the menus, and in the car its recorded engine and its sampled tyres, kerbs, contact, start lights and passing cars, played when the game plays its own. The Sound menu at the top of the page leaves out the A500's muffling filter ("Amiga, no filter") or switches to the PC version's AdLib sound.
  • Before the menus the game asks which language your manual is in, then for a word from it: page 85, paragraph 4, line 1, word 11, which is require. The page answers both for you, and picks keyboard control.
  • You can switch between modern and classic with the Style menu at the top of the page. The Shape menu makes the new view 4:3, as the game's screen, so the cockpit's sides show; the modern style is wide. The Cockpit menu draws the game's cockpit and dash smoothed, with a filter made for pixel art, or as the game's square pixels. The Screen menu switches back to the game's own drawing.
  • What you save in the game (Load/Save Game in its main menu: saved games, names, track records, car setups) and its options are kept in this browser and come back on your next visit. The Saves button at the top of the page lists them and can forget them. The page's menu choices are kept too.

How it works

The game runs in js-dos, DOSBox compiled to WebAssembly. The page reads the cars, the camera and the track from the emulated PC's memory every frame and draws them with WebGL, using the game's own projection, colours and drawing rules, which were decoded from the program. The source and the notes are on GitHub.

Because the page does the 3D drawing, the game has time to spare: it runs at 30 frames a second, twice its usual rate, on a slower emulated processor. The page draws at your screen's rate and blends between the game's frames.
